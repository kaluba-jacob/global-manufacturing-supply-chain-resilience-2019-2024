# Business Analysis Report
## Global Manufacturing Supply Chain Restructuring & Resilience (2019–2024)

---

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes how global manufacturing trade flows have shifted in the aftermath of COVID-19, geopolitical tensions and the rise of "friend-shoring" / "near-shoring" policies. Using UN Comtrade bilateral trade data, OECD TiVA indicators and World Bank Logistics Performance Index (LPI) data for 2019–2023, we find that:

- Global manufacturing supply chains have **not** broadly regionalized. Most sectors are still globalizing.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa** has shifted its import dependence away from Europe and toward East Asia.
- Countries with stronger logistics infrastructure have **less concentrated** (more diversified) supply sources.
- Intra-African manufacturing trade has **declined**, despite the AfCFTA opportunity.

---

## 1. Introduction

The 2019–2024 period has been one of the most turbulent eras for global supply chains: the COVID-19 pandemic exposed vulnerabilities, the war in Ukraine disrupted energy and food markets, and major economies (US, EU) explicitly promoted friend-shoring and near-shoring.

This report addresses three business questions:

1. How have manufacturing trade flows shifted since 2019?
2. Which industries regionalized, and which stayed global?
3. How does logistics performance affect supply chain concentration?

---

## 2. Q1 — Trade Flow Shifts

### Finding
Between 2019 and the latest available year (2023), the largest trade shifts were observed in **Sub-Saharan Africa**:

| Partner region | Change in import share |
|----------------|------------------------|
| East Asia & Pacific | **+6.62 pp** |
| Europe & Central Asia | **−5.63 pp** |

### Interpretation
African buyers are diversifying away from traditional European suppliers toward cheaper East Asian manufacturing (notably China, Vietnam). This is consistent with the broader "China+1" and Asian manufacturing rise narrative.

---

## 3. Q2 — Sectoral Regionalization

### Finding
We measured regionalization as the share of imports sourced within the same region as the importer. Across 11 manufacturing sectors:

- **Only 2 sectors regionalized**: Wood & pulp (+0.96 pp), Footwear (+0.89 pp)
- **9 sectors became more globalized**, not less

### Interpretation
The narrative of broad supply chain regionalization is **not supported by trade data** for most manufacturing. Heavy, low-value, or regulation-sensitive sectors (wood, footwear) regionalized modestly, but machinery, electronics, chemicals, and automotive parts remain globally sourced.

---

## 4. Q3 — Logistics Infrastructure and Concentration

### Finding
- Correlation between LPI (logistics performance) and HHI (partner concentration): **r = −0.474**, p < 0.001
- Regression confirms: a 1-point increase in LPI is associated with a statistically significant decrease in HHI.

### Interpretation
Better logistics infrastructure (ports, customs, inland connectivity, timeliness) allows countries to **diversify** suppliers rather than depend on one dominant partner. Logistics is a resilience lever, not just a cost factor.

---

## 5. Q4 — Deep Dive: Africa

### Findings
- Intra-African manufacturing trade share fell from **6.03% (2019)** to **4.88% (2023)**.
- African average HHI is **above the global average**, meaning African importers depend more on a small number of foreign suppliers.
- African LPI scores are among the lowest globally, consistent with the Q3 relationship.

### Implications for African Manufacturing Supply Chains

1. **Trade diversification is already happening, but away from Africa, not toward it.** African importers now buy more from East Asia and less from Europe. Intra-African trade is shrinking.

2. **Logistics infrastructure is the binding constraint.** The Q3 result implies that investing in ports (Mombasa, Djibouti, Abidjan, Lagos), customs digitization, and regional corridors (Lagos–Abidjan, Northern Corridor) would directly reduce supply chain concentration and price volatility.

3. **The AfCFTA opportunity is underused.** Without targeted logistics investment, tariff liberalization alone will not reverse the intra-African trade decline.

4. **Friend-shoring is an opportunity, not a threat.** As Western buyers diversify away from China, African countries with better LPI could capture labor-intensive manufacturing (footwear, assembly).

---

## 6. Conclusion

The 2019–2023 data does not show a broad retreat from globalization in manufacturing. Instead, it shows a **redirection**: trade is shifting along geopolitical lines, and the countries that benefit most are those with strong logistics. For African economies, the policy agenda is clear: invest in logistics infrastructure to diversify suppliers and make the AfCFTA economically effective.
